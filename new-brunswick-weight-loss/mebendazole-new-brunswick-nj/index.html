    How Mebendazole Treatment Works in New Brunswick, NJ

    How Mebendazole Works in New Brunswick

    Mebendazole blocks a worm's ability to take in glucose by disrupting tiny structures inside its cells called microtubules. The worms starve and die. Dead worms leave in the stool over the next few days, often without being seen, and that is completely expected. Only a small amount is absorbed into the blood. In the US, it is sold as an FDA-approved chewable tablet.

    Who Mebendazole Is For

    Adults usually ask for it because of pinworm. Nighttime anal itching, especially when kids at home have the same complaint, is the classic sign. Roundworm, whipworm and hookworm respond to it too, and those usually follow travel. Adults with a positive test qualify. It is not a detox and has no role in weight loss.

    Mebendazole Dosing and Schedule in New Brunswick

    For pinworm, the usual plan is a single 100 mg chewable tablet, repeated in two weeks. That second dose catches worms that hatched from eggs the first dose could not reach. Other intestinal worms are often treated with 100 mg twice daily for three days, followed by a stool test in some cases. Chew it fully. You can take it with or without food.

    What to Expect After Taking Mebendazole

    Most people feel nothing after the dose. A few notice mild cramping or loose stools for a day. Itching usually eases within a few days, and it can take another week for the skin to calm down completely, especially if it was scratched a lot. You may not see worms. Put the two week dose in your phone calendar the day you take the first.

    Side Effects of Mebendazole in New Brunswick

    Because so little is absorbed, mebendazole is well tolerated at short courses. Belly pain, loose stools and nausea are the usual complaints. Rash is rare. Longer, high-dose courses used for other conditions carry added risks, like lower white blood cell counts or liver effects, which is why providers stick to standard dosing here. Report fever or a spreading rash the same day.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake asks about symptoms, timing, household members, recent travel and any test results. The provider will ask about pregnancy. Mebendazole is generally avoided in the first trimester, and the provider also screens for breastfeeding, liver disease and interacting drugs. Taking metronidazole at the same time has been tied to rare but serious skin reactions. Non-pinworm infections usually need a stool test first.

    How Mebendazole Compares to Other Options in New Brunswick

    Albendazole works the same way and is sometimes used for the same infections. Pyrantel pamoate, sold without a prescription, also treats pinworm by paralyzing the worms. For pinworm, ivermectin is not the first pick. If you are not sure what you have, the antiparasitic protocol helps sort out which medicine fits before anything is prescribed. Your provider recommends the drug with the best evidence.
